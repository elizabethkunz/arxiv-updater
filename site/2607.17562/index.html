Temporal Diffraction Grating for Engineered Superconducting Qubit Dissipation

arXiv:2607.17562 PDF HTML
Summary
We demonstrate that pulsing a parametrically activated Purcell decay channel in a flux-tunable transmon qubit creates a temporal diffraction grating in the dissipation spectrum. Pulsed on/off modulation reorganizes the qubit's decay into interference fringes analogous to N-slit Fraunhofer diffraction, with each modulation window acting as a temporal aperture whose spacing, contrast, and envelope are controlled by the pulse duration and duty cycle.
Motivation
Dissipation engineering in superconducting circuits traditionally relies on static spectral shaping or continuous coherent drives, but time-domain control of loss channels offers a complementary control knob for open quantum system dynamics.
Method
We apply periodic on/off gating to a parametric flux modulation ($\omega_m \approx 1.308$ GHz, $g_{\mathrm{eff}}/2\pi = 0.14$ MHz) that couples a transmon ($\omega_{q,\mathrm{on}}/2\pi = 4.259$ GHz) to a readout resonator ($\omega_r/2\pi = 6.8759$ GHz, $\kappa/2\pi = 0.375$ MHz) and measure the excited-state probability as a function of modulation detuning while varying $\tau_{\mathrm{on}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{off}}$. We model the dynamics using a Chebyshev-polynomial propagator formalism for repeated on/off blocks.
Result
Central interference dips are spaced by $\delta(\Delta_m/2\pi) = 1/(2t_c)$ where $t_c = \tau_{\mathrm{on}} + \tau_{\mathrm{off}}$ is the cycle time; the measured spectra match simulations from Eq. Abstract
Parametric frequency modulation is a standard tool in superconducting circuits for activating tunable interactions and implementing quantum gates. Here, we engineer dissipation in a flux-tunable transmon qubit by using sideband modulation to bring it into resonance with a lossy resonator, opening an on-demand Purcell decay channel. We find that pulsing this channel on and off does not simply lower the time-averaged decay rate; instead, it reorganizes the dissipation spectrum into a structured interference pattern. A Chebyshev-propagator model for the repeated on/off block reproduces the measured spectra and reveals a close structural correspondence to N-slit Fraunhofer diffraction, with each on-window acting as a temporal aperture. By varying the pulse duration and duty cycle, we demonstrate control over the spacing, contrast, and envelope of the dissipation spectrum. These results establish pulsed parametric modulation as a direct method for shaping engineered dissipation in superconducting circuits and provide a new control knob for open quantum system dynamics.
